🏗️ Formation

We use comparative adjectives to compare two people, animals, or things.

short adjective + er + than

Example: Ali is taller than Omar. 🧒

Short adjectives

A short adjective has only one syllable (one "beat" when you say it): short, fast, tall, clean, nice, big, hot ...

The 4 spelling rules

1) Most short adjectives → just add -er then than

AdjectiveComparative
shortshorter than
cleancleaner than
fastfaster than
talltaller than

Example: My room is cleaner than my sister's room. 🛏️

2) Adjectives ending in "e" → just add -r then than

AdjectiveComparative
safesafer than
nicenicer than

Example: Cats are safer than lions. 🐱

3) Adjectives ending in a consonant + "y" → remove the y, add -ier then than

AdjectiveComparative
heavyheavier than
healthyhealthier than

Example: Fruits are healthier than sweets. 🍎

4) One-syllable adjectives ending in one vowel + one consonant → double the consonant, add -er then than

AdjectiveComparative
bigbigger than
hothotter than

Example: Summer is hotter than spring. ☀️

📌 A quick way to remember: look at the LAST letters of the adjective to choose the right rule — plain consonant → -er, silent e → -r, consonant+y → -ier, vowel+consonant → double the consonant + -er.

🎯 Usage

We use comparative adjectives whenever we compare two things, people, or animals — never just one, and never three or more (that would need a superlative like "the biggest").

Example: The elephant is bigger than the dog. 🐘🐶

Example: The rabbit is faster than the turtle. 🐇🐢

🔑 Key Word

The word than almost always appears after a comparative adjective, right before the second thing being compared.

than

Example: Ahmed is shorter than Amr.
